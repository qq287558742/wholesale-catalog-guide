Margin After Returns, Not Margin on the Label

Published by DPJ Wholesale. Return rate is a cost of goods, not a customer-service metric.

The one-line version

A category returning 20% of units needs a materially different margin than one returning 4%. Most assortment advice treats returns as an afterthought; in our data it is one of the strongest predictors of whether a line survives two seasons.

What reorders despite a high return rate

Almost nothing. High-return categories can still be profitable, but they need either a price point that absorbs the return or a fit guide good enough to cut the rate — both are real work, and a small retailer usually has budget for only one or two such categories.

The practical version

Split the buy three ways: proven reorders (buy deep), first-time SKUs (buy narrow and wide), and seasonal or fashion lines (treat as a bet with a deadline). Stop a SKU when its reorder rate falls below the cost of the shelf space it occupies.
